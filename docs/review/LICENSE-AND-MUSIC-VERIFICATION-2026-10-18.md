## 1. Method and access log

Method: `curl` with a browser User-Agent, `--compressed`, through the sandbox proxy; raw upstream files from `raw.githubusercontent.com`, registry JSON from `pypi.org` and `registry.npmjs.org`, vendor and platform HTML converted to text. The `WebFetch` tool could not resolve several hosts (DNS `ENOTFOUND` for `www.epidemicsound.com`, `ai.google.dev`, `www.cstr.ed.ac.uk`), so `curl` was used instead. Repo lockfiles were read only (`grep`), not changed.

## 3. Findings by topic and the decisions they change

### 3.1 Piper is usable server-side under GPL, but the default voices carry dataset-license risk (V-03 to V-06)

The code-license picture in the sweep holds: Piper (piper1-gpl), espeak-ng and phonemizer are all GPL-3.0-or-later, read from upstream files. The new finding is on **voices**: Piper's own docs say it is "intended for personal use and text to speech research only", the `ryan` voice dataset is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial; do not use on a monetized stream), and the `lessac` dataset (basis of the default US English voice, and of the fine-tuned `amy` and `ryan` voices) comes with a research-only licence that excludes "any commercial purpose". Whether that restricts a trained model is not answered by any page read.

Decision it changes: AUDIO-TTS should name the exact Piper voices it plans to ship and record each MODEL_CARD license in `docs/legal/THIRD-PARTY.md` before the engine is chosen. A listening test should include only voices whose dataset license permits commercial use (for example `libritts_r`, CC BY 4.0, which needs attribution). Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0 weights, CC BY training audio listed on its card) is easier to clear, but its Python dependency chain pulls GPL phonemizer/espeak-ng via `kokoro-onnx` (V-14), so the isolation requirement stands.

### 3.2 Epidemic Sound: the livestream question is still open; the "listening experience" rule is official and explicit (V-80 to V-92)

Verified: livestream use on Twitch is advertised ("unlimited use of our catalog while streaming"); content published during an active subscription stays cleared forever; new content after cancellation is monetized by Epidemic; safelisting is per channel and needs an active subscription; the MCP server exists, is in Claude's directory since 2026-08-31, and works with a personal API key under an active paid subscription.

Not answered by any primary page: whether a continuous 24/7 broadcast that began during the subscription is "published content" after a lapse, and whether archived streams count as VOD (Pro and Business exclude "Video on Demand (VOD), Streaming VOD"). The "listening experience" article is explicit that music "where visuals/narration are secondary to the music" is not allowed under any license, naming playlists and online radio. Twitch's own rules (V-96) separately ban "radio station-style" broadcasts without a live visual performance.

Decisions it changes: (1) Keep the written-confirmation requirement from Epidemic support as a gate, with three precise questions: 24/7 unattended; VOD archive of a livestream; lapse behaviour. (2) The sim must be the visual and the music must stay secondary (never a music-only scene). (3) Plan choice: Creator is described as "non-commercial" while also allowing monetization of one channel per platform (V-80), which Epidemic's own pages contradict each other on; if the Hiveplex channel is monetized, ask whether Creator suffices or whether Pro (3 channels, client work, digital ads) is needed. "Apps & games" coverage appears only from **Business Plus**; ask whether a live game stream with a game-like overlay is an "app or game" for licensing. (4) The MCP server can be wired behind the `MusicSource` adapter; the MCP Terms of Service could not be read (V-91), so automated selection is allowed by Epidemic's marketing ("Using the MCP Server doesn't change any of your licensing or usage terms") but not yet confirmed in the legal text. Prices must be read in a browser (V-87).

### 3.3 Twitch and YouTube treatment of long streams (V-94 to V-99)

Twitch bans "Radio Station-Style Broadcast" of recorded music without a live visual performance and can mute VODs and delete Clips on audio recognition; three strikes ends the account. YouTube scans all live streams, may replace the stream with a placeholder, and interrupts the stream unless the rights holder has added the channel to their Content ID allowlist, even if the music is licensed. Decision it changes: for any licensed library used on YouTube Live, safelisting (the allowlist step) is a pre-launch checklist item; a fallback no-music scene should be one command away; keep the music cue sheet. The sweep's statement that Soundtrack by Twitch is unavailable is not supported by Twitch's own page (V-95) and should be removed unless a primary source is found.

### 3.4 Free sources (V-100 to V-105)

Kenney (CC0, optional credit), Incompetech (CC BY 4.0; livestream credit rules spelled out) and the YouTube Audio Library (Content-ID safe on YouTube only; CC tracks need credit) are verified. Freesound and Pixabay need a manual read in a browser. Decision: use Kenney CC0 for SFX first; treat Incompetech as acceptable only with an on-stream or profile credit plan; do not rely on the YouTube Audio Library for the Twitch side without separate confirmation.

### 3.5 Direct dependencies and lockfile flags (V-20 to V-42)

All the MIT/Apache/BSD claims for discord.py, twitchio, pydantic, fastapi, uvicorn, websockets, milsymbol, Detoxify, and the Fontsource fonts hold. Corrections: litellm has an `enterprise/` directory under a different license (V-23); TikTokLive's license has bespoke added terms, not just "AGPL" (V-25); psycopg is LGPL-3.0 (verified). Lockfile flags resolve as follows: `mypy-extensions` is MIT upstream (the open flag in LS §2.4 can be closed), `typing-extensions` is PSF-2.0, `minimatch` is BlueOak-1.0.0. The two licenses not on the Q-LEGAL-1 allowlist (PSF-2.0, BlueOak-1.0.0) are both permissive and confirmed; adding them to the allowlist is the owner's call.

### 3.6 Tools (V-50 to V-58)

OBS (GPL v2 or later), Kdenlive (GPLv3 text), Krita (GPLv3 text), Inkscape (GPL 2+ source, v3+ binaries), Audacity (GPLv3) all confirmed as GPL-family applications run, not linked. Reaper: $60 discounted (personal, or revenue up to $20,000, or education/non-profit) and $225 commercial, with a 60-day free trial. DaVinci Resolve: free is capped at 8-bit, 60 fps, UHD; Studio is $295 (not "$295-299"); the free version does include multi-user collaboration. Decision: Resolve free is enough for 1080p devlogs; if the channel's gross revenue grows past $20,000, Reaper needs the $225 tier.

### 3.7 Moderation and analytics tools (V-60 to V-77)

Perspective API sunsets after 2026-12-31 (verified from the Jigsaw page): do not adopt. OpenAI moderation is documented as free; the "paid from 7 August" claim has no support on the official page, so it should be dropped or treated as a rumor, but the ModerationProvider should still be replaceable. Detoxify is Apache-2.0. Llama Guard 4 is under the Llama 4 Community License ("Built with Llama" display duty; a 700 million MAU threshold). Analytics: GoAccess MIT, Umami MIT (cloud Hobby tier is free to 100K events/month), Plausible CE AGPL, Matomo GPL v3+, GoatCounter modified EUPL 1.2 with a hosted free tier "for reasonable public usage" that explicitly allows small-to-medium businesses. The CNIL page confirms an opt-out regime for strictly configured audience measurement; it does not support the "25 months" or "list retired" statements in the sweep.

### 3.8 Military symbology (V-106, V-107)

MIL-STD-2525D is marked "Distribution A: Approved for public release, distribution is unlimited" on its cover, and the foreword says it is "available for use by non-DOD entities". That supports drawing symbols with milsymbol (MIT code) and citing the standard by name. It is a distribution marking, not a statement about third-party material in the PDF, and it says nothing about NATO APP-6, whose terms remain unread (NSO blocked).

### 3.9 Gemini TTS (V-110 to V-116)

The reported facts hold and are now primary: GA date, voice-design endpoint, 200-voice and 1-year TTL limit, and both price tiers. Two corrections: text input also doubles in 2027, and the PLATFORM-REALITY "$0.015 per minute" figure should read $0.009 to $0.0135 per minute (2026) and $0.018 to $0.027 (2027). Decision it changes: none to the hybrid plan; the live-line budget math in the GT doc is confirmed. Store the voice description prompts (the 1-year TTL is verified).
