## 1. Voice agreements and voice-over guidance

### 1.1 NAVA "Artificial Intelligence Rider" Ver. 4.5 (updated 4.14.26)
- URL: https://navavoices.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/NAVA-AI-Rider-Ver-4.5-4.14.26.docx
- What: a one-page rider attached to a talent/client contract that overrides conflicting text. Read in full (docx text extracted).
- Publisher: National Association of Voice Actors (NAVA) [publisher inferred from domain and filename footer "NAVA_AI_SYNTH_RIDER"].
- Reuse terms: not stated in the document (no licence line in body or footer). Do not copy text.
- Ideas to borrow:
  1. Purpose limit: the client may not use the recording "for purposes other than those specified in the initial Agreement", with cloning and AI training named as examples.
  2. Blended or composite voices are covered: no generated voice that "contains data from Talent even if the generated voice is not recognizable". Closes the "it no longer sounds like them" loophole.
  3. No selling, transferring or contracting out the files for AI use (text-to-speech, speech-to-speech, training) without the talent's knowledge and consent, plus a duty to keep files secure (encryption if in the cloud).
- Note: its consent test is "knowledge and consent", not "separate written consent and payment", so our §6 is stricter on payment.

### 1.2 AAVA Voice Actor Generative AI Agreement (via Equity NZ listing)
- URL of listing: https://equity.org.nz/nzava-association-of-voice-artists/ (the AAVA document itself was not located or read).
- What: a fillable PDF usable as a standalone agreement or contract addendum; per the NZ page it replaced an earlier "AI Rider" and was created with the Arts Law Centre of Australia [UNVERIFIED: the Arts Law and replacement details came from a search summary, not the page I fetched].
- Publisher of listing: Equity New Zealand with NZAVA; page "last updated 16/06/2025".
- Reuse terms: the listing says the AAVA agreement is "Free to use, share with clients, and adapt". That is a second-hand statement; verify on the AAVA site (aavavoices.org [UNVERIFIED]) before reuse. The Equity page itself states no licence.
- Ideas to borrow (from the page's checklist, not the agreement text):
  1. A contract should have explicit usage limits on synthetic voice and machine training, term limits or opt-out, and secure storage and tracking of recordings and AI products.
  2. Search-word list of red-flag terms in any template we adopt or counter-sign: "digital double", "voice modeling", "machine learning", "synthesization", "simulation".
  3. Use as a pattern: standalone addendum so the AI promise can be shown to a lawyer and contributor separately.

### 1.3 MEAA "Protecting performers' image and work from unauthorised AI use"
- URL: https://www.meaa.org/news/protecting-performers-image-and-work-from-unauthorised-ai-use/
- What: union news/guidance post with suggested contract clauses. Published 3 Oct 2024 by Media, Entertainment & Arts Alliance (Australia).
- Reuse terms: not stated (page has a general copyright footer). Do not copy.
- Ideas:
  1. Restrict use to "that production" (our licence is limited to Operation Nexus use).
  2. Producer agrees not to use image or recordings for AI (digital doubles, synthetic recordings, machine learning) without permission, with secure storage.
  3. Limits on media type and duration; MEAA "discourages in-perpetuity use". This directly conflicts with our perpetual licence. Expect pushback from union-aware actors.

### 1.4 SAG-AFTRA Interactive Media Agreement (2025) coverage
- URL: https://www.thewrap.com/sag-aftra-video-game-deal-details/ (news article, TheWrap, 12 June 2025). SAG-AFTRA's own page returned HTTP 403, so the primary text was not read.
- What: press summary of the union video-game deal. Only for union members, so not our contract, but it shows the industry norm.
- Reuse terms: news article, not reusable; contract text not read.
- Ideas:
  1. "Informed consent" for each planned AI use of voice, likeness and movement, not a blanket grant.
  2. Separate, higher pay for replica use (a 7.5x scale minimum for "real-time generation").
  3. Consent can be suspended during a strike. Not relevant to us, but shows consent is treated as revocable in union deals.
- Also seen only in a search summary [UNVERIFIED]: a January 2024 SAG-AFTRA and Replica Studios deal with consent, minimum rates, limited replica duration and storage rules.

## 2. Artist commission and work-for-hire vs licence

### 2.1 Legal Moves PC, "Work-for-Hire for Game Studios: Why Your Contractor Might Still Own Your Art"
- URL: https://legalmoveslawfirm.com/work-for-hire-agreement/
- What: law-firm article by Zachary Strebeck (video game attorney), dated 20 June 2026.
- Reuse: "© 2026 Legal Moves PC. All rights reserved", informational only. Do not copy.
- Ideas:
  1. Work-for-hire only works for nine statutory categories plus a signed writing before work starts; game art can fit (audiovisual work) but standalone assets often do not. Supports our choice to avoid relying on work-for-hire.
  2. Use "hereby assigns" (present transfer), not "agrees to assign". Relevant only if we ever take assignment; we are licensing.
  3. Assignments carry a 35-year termination right under 17 U.S.C. 203 [the article says this; verify with counsel]. A licence structure has its own termination questions (see gaps).
  4. An email or invoice note gets "a revocable license at most", so the licence should be written, signed, and say irrevocable/perpetual (subject to our §10 suspension).

### 2.2 Commission Contract Template v3.0 (itch.io devlog)
- URL: https://jnbutlerart.itch.io/commission-contract-template/devlog/299364/commission-contract-template-v30

Useful as a pattern: an artist keeps rights and offers tiered licences. Everything else about its clauses is [UNVERIFIED].

## 4. Credits page and credit removal

### 4.1 IGDA Game Crediting policies (Game Credits SIG)
- URLs: https://igda.org/sigs/credits/ (read), news: https://80.lv/articles/igda-updates-its-game-crediting-policies/ (80 Level, 12 Apr 2023).
- What: industry guidelines (Full Policies updated 13 Mar 2023; Guidelines 10.1 PDF). I did not read the guide itself.
- Reuse: no licence stated on the page.
- Ideas: (1) a written credit policy exists as a reference to cite; (2) a 30-day or 5% contribution threshold to earn a credit is reported in news [UNVERIFIED, from search summaries only]; (3) the 80 Level piece reports 83.1% of survey respondents said their employer or client had no credits policy, so stating ours in the contract is already better than the norm. Page says nothing on credit removal.

### 4.2 itch.io community threads (search summaries only, [UNVERIFIED])
Indicate that aliases and pseudonyms are normal in credits, that privacy is a reason to omit a legal name, and that credit disputes can escalate (a Workers & Resources: Soviet Republic story involved DMCA claims and a Steam page takedown; one side's account only). Supports: written credit terms, alias allowed, a fix window. No document found on credit removal. Our removal-for-new-content rule has no public precedent I could find.

## 5. Open-source contributor agreements

### 5.1 Apache Individual Contributor License Agreement V2.2
- URLs: https://www.apache.org/licenses/icla.pdf (read in full), https://www.apache.org/licenses/contributor-agreements.html (read).
- What: contributors keep ownership and license to the Foundation. Published by the Apache Software Foundation; the contributor-agreements page footer says "Licensed under the Apache License, Version 2.0". The ICLA PDF itself states no licence [not stated], so reuse of its text is unconfirmed; ask or use Harmony instead.
- Ideas:
  1. Opening statement that the contributor "reserve[s] all right, title, and interest" except the licence granted; and that nothing changes the contributor's own use. Matches our §4.
  2. Originality plus disclosure: the contributor says the work is their own creation and lists any third-party licence or restriction they know of; separate marking for work "submitted on behalf of a third-party". Matches our §8 and could improve the third-party list.
  3. Duty to notify if a representation later becomes untrue; "AS IS" without warranties by the contributor; no obligation to support. Plus a statement that the contributor has the right to grant the licence, including employer permission.
  4. Licence words to compare with ours: "perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, no-charge, royalty-free, irrevocable" and a list of rights (reproduce, derivative works, publicly display, perform, sublicense, distribute). Note it grants sublicensing; ours does not say.
- Caveat: the ICLA is non-exclusive, free, and irrevocable; ours is paid, partly exclusive and suspendable. Structure helps, terms differ.

### 5.2 Project Harmony agreements
- URL: https://harmonyagreements.org/ (read, but template pages not read).
- What: community project publishing standard FOSS contributor agreement templates, including licence-type and assignment-type options per the search summary [UNVERIFIED]. Page says "Content on this site is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 Unported License", unclear whether this covers the template files.
- Ideas: offers a menu of licence vs assignment options, and its own guide explains the choices; critics (a GNOME blog in the search results) separate the useful originality certification from the riskier rights grant [UNVERIFIED, second-hand]. Reuse: possibly CC BY 3.0; confirm on its /agreements pages.

## Gap list: findings vs our draft (max 10)
1. No third-party transfer or sale clause: NAVA's rider bars passing files to others for AI use. Our §6 says "allow anyone else" but does not cover subcontractors, hosts or platforms scraping content. Check platforms' own AI terms (Twitch, YouTube) [lawyer].
2. No data-security duty for stored recordings (NAVA, Equity NZ, Replica terms). Add a short promise.
3. Blended/composite voice not mentioned. §6 says "synthetic copy" of the voice; NAVA also bars generated voices that merely contain the contributor's data.
4. Perpetual licence (§5, D3) runs against MEAA's advice against in-perpetuity use. Likely to be negotiated; consider whether a long fixed term is acceptable. Owner decision.
5. Sublicensing and assignability: §5 lists hosting services but not an express sublicence right; Apache's grant includes sublicensing. Also clarify that the licence survives §14 transfer. Lawyer.
6. Irrevocability: §10 suspends the licence for unpaid work, while Legal Moves warns unwritten or loose deals are "revocable". State whether the licence is irrevocable apart from §10. Lawyer; also ask about US termination rights for licences/transfers (§203 is said to apply to grants by authors) [UNVERIFIED applicability].
7. Third-party material: add Apache-style disclosure of any third-party licences and a duty to notify if a representation becomes untrue.
8. Unusual: credit removal limited to new content; public tools I found do not address it, so test wording with the lawyer. Also unusual: our consent exception requires separate payment; NAVA requires only consent.
9. Unusual: a ban on contributor use of generative AI (§8, D6) is stricter than any template found and needs a plain way to check compliance and a defined remedy.
10. Missing: what happens to a work the Project does not use or reject; moral rights waiver or consent to edits for non-US contributors; what happens to confidential material if the contributor dies or the Project ends. Lawyer.

## Only a lawyer should decide
Enforceability of exclusivity and perpetual terms; US and non-US termination rights and moral rights; whether licence plus "no AI" is better than assignment; indemnity and liability caps; governing law and venue; tax and 1099 or W-8 treatment, contractor classification, minors and age verification; the owner signing under a pseudonym; privacy law for contributor data; electronic signature and email-amendment validity; whether a union-member performer can sign this at all; whether any text here may be reused (licence terms were mostly not stated).
